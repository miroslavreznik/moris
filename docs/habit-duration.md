# Habit duration

Duration is independent of repetition: daily, every N days, selected weekdays,
and flexible weekly targets all support an ongoing plan, a number of days,
weeks or calendar months, or a chosen end date.

- The end date is inclusive. Three calendar months from 2026-11-18 runs through
  2027-02-17; the anniversary starts the next period.
- Pauses keep the original end date. They exclude paused days from scheduling
  without extending the plan automatically.
- After the end date, a habit leaves Today and reminder eligibility. Progress
  keeps its history and earned XP in the collapsed **Ended habits** section.
- An ended period does not imply every scheduled opportunity was completed.
  Historical corrections remain available within the planned dates.
- Editing the end date extends the same continuous habit lifetime. Extending
  an ended plan can bring intervening scheduled days back into its history.
- Existing habits stay ongoing. Backup format 4 preserves optional end dates;
  formats 1–3 remain importable with no end date. No database reset is needed.

## Research behind the expandable note

The [2024 systematic review](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9032/12/23/2488) included
20 studies; only four directly reported habit-formation times. Those studies
reported different measures; where medians were reported, estimates were
59–66 days, with substantial variation between people and behaviours. This is
not a personal prediction or guaranteed deadline.

The [2023 gym and handwashing study](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2216115120)
found that gym routines developed over months. It inferred habits from
context-sensitive behavioural predictability, rather than directly measuring
automaticity. Its method differs from the self-report measures in the review.
Daily-habit estimates do not establish an exact timeline for a twice-weekly goal.
The app treats the end date as a point to review the plan, without declaring that
a habit has become automatic.
